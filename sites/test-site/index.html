The Movie

About the Film

Comedy Satire Cult Classic Mike Judge

Office Space is a 1999 American workplace comedy written and directed by Mike Judge, based on his earlier animated Milton shorts. The film stars Ron Livingston as Peter Gibbons, a disgruntled software engineer at the fictional Initech Corporation who is stuck in a soul-crushing cubicle job with overbearing bosses and meaningless TPS reports.

After a hypnotherapy session goes unexpectedly sideways — the hypnotherapist dies mid-session, leaving Peter in a state of blissful indifference — Peter simply stops caring about work altogether. His newfound attitude paradoxically impresses corporate downsizers, earning him a promotion while his friends Michael Bolton and Samir Nagheenanajar face layoffs.

In retaliation, the trio hatches a scheme to plant a virus in Initech's accounting system to siphon fractions of pennies into their own bank account. A rounding error causes the virus to steal over $300,000 — far more than intended. Meanwhile, the long-suffering Milton, whose stapler has been confiscated one too many times, takes matters into his own hands.

Released to modest box office returns, Office Space became a massive hit on home video and cable television, cementing its status as one of the defining cult comedies of its generation — and an enduring mirror held up to corporate America.

Did You Know?

Fun Facts

🎬

The film originated as a series of animated shorts about the character Milton that Mike Judge created for Saturday Night Live and Liquid Television in the early 1990s.

💰

Office Space was a box office disappointment on release, grossing just $12.2 million on a $10 million budget — but became a massive cult hit through DVD and cable television.

📷

The printer destruction scene — set to "Still" by Geto Boys — was filmed in an actual abandoned building and took multiple takes. The crew genuinely enjoyed smashing the printer.

🥪

Swingline did not make a red stapler at the time of filming. After the movie's cult success, they began producing them to meet overwhelming fan demand.

🎶

Actor David Herman (Michael Bolton) had to convince director Mike Judge he could convincingly lip-sync rap songs. The Geto Boys scenes became some of the film's most beloved moments.

🏢

The Initech office was filmed in Austin, Texas, where Mike Judge was based. Judge insisted on authentic cubicle culture — many crew members said it felt uncomfortably familiar.

👔

Gary Cole based Bill Lumbergh's infuriating delivery on a real person — a corporate type he had observed whose tone made every request sound simultaneously polite and soul-crushing.

✌️

The concept of "pieces of flair" has entered the cultural lexicon as shorthand for any pointless, mandated display of enthusiasm — a testament to the film's lasting resonance.
